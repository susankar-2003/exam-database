1. To "Rake over the coals" means:

A) To clean a fireplace
B) To scold or reprimand severely
C) To cook food on charcoal
D) To search for gold mines
Ans: B

2. "Read the riot act" means:

A) To study criminal law books
B) To issue a stern warning or reprimand
C) To start a public protest
D) To announce election results
Ans: B

3. "Rule of thumb" refers to:

A) A law measured by fingers
B) A practical rule based on experience rather than theory
C) A strict judicial punishment
D) An official government rule
Ans: B

4. To "See eye to eye" means:

A) To stare into someone's eyes
B) To agree fully with someone
C) To have good visual eyesight
D) To confront an opponent directly
Ans: B

5. "Shed crocodile tears" means:

A) To cry tears of genuine sorrow
B) To display insincere or false sorrow
C) To weep uncontrollably
D) To express anger through tears
Ans: B

6. To "Sit on the fence" means:

A) To rest outdoors
B) To avoid taking a side in a dispute
C) To build a wooden boundary
D) To watch a game from height
Ans: B

7. A "Skeleton in the closet" means:

A) A medical display model
B) A shameful secret kept hidden
C) An old ghost story
D) A clean storage space
Ans: B

8. "Spick and span" means:

A) Very old and worn out
B) Extremely neat, clean, and tidy
C) Scattered and untidy
D) Quick and energetic
Ans: B

9. To "Steal someone's thunder" means:

A) To create bad weather
B) To take credit for someone else's ideas or accomplishments
C) To make loud noises
D) To perform a magic trick
Ans: B

10. A "Straw in the wind" indicates:

A) Dry weather conditions
B) A small sign of what might happen in the future
C) Farming activities
D) A weak building foundation
Ans: B

11. "Take the bull by the horns" means:

A) To fight animals in a arena
B) To deal with a difficult situation directly and bravely
C) To run away from danger
D) To catch a stray animal
Ans: B

12. To "Through the grapevine" means:

A) From fruit orchards
B) Through informal rumors or gossip
C) From official news bulletins
D) By sending formal letters
Ans: B

13. "Throw in the towel" means:

A) To do laundry work
B) To admit defeat and give up
C) To dry oneself after a bath
D) To clean a dirty surface
Ans: B

14. To "Throw dust in someone's eyes" means:

A) To cause eye irritation physically
B) To deceive or mislead someone
C) To sweep a dusty floor
D) To blindfold a person
Ans: B

15. "To the nines" (as in 'dressed to the nines') means:

A) Dressed in simple clothes
B) Dressed very elegantly and elaborately
C) Dressed in nine different colors
D) Wearing old uniform
Ans: B

16. A "Tooth and nail" fight means:

A) Fighting using sharp tools
B) Fighting with great fierce determination and energy
C) A mild argument
D) A game of biting
Ans: B

17. To "Under the weather" means:

A) Sitting out in the open rain
B) Feeling slightly unwell or sick
C) Enjoying cool breeze
D) Working in bad climate
Ans: B

18. "Up in arms" means:

A) Raising hands in celebration
B) Angry and protesting strongly against something
C) Carrying weapons in battle
D) Hugging someone tightly
Ans: B

19. "Up in the air" means:

A) Flying in an airplane
B) Unresolved or uncertain
C) Very high in elevation
D) Blown away by wind
Ans: B

20. "Vanity Fair" refers to:

A) A local trade exhibition
B) A place or world of empty foolish luxury and show
C) A beauty contest
D) A religious festival
Ans: B

21. "Wash one's hands of" means:

A) To clean hands with soap
B) To refuse to take further responsibility for something
C) To perform a ritual before eating
D) To finish doing household chores
Ans: B

22. "Wear one's heart on one's sleeve" means:

A) To have a heart tattoo
B) To show one's emotions openly and freely
C) To hide feelings from others
D) To wear red colored shirts
Ans: B

23. A "Wet blanket" is a person who:

A) Cleans wet clothes
B) Spoils other people's fun and enthusiasm
C) Works in rainy weather
D) Provides warm bedding
Ans: B

24. "When pigs fly" is an idiom used to describe something that:

A) Will happen very soon
B) Will never happen
C) Happens in animal farms
D) Is scientifically proven
Ans: B

25. "Zero hour" refers to:

A) Midnight 12:00 AM
B) The exact scheduled time for an action to begin
C) A period of no work
